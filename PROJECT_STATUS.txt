- V and N buildings go to NewCo; S remains with RemainCo.

  with no emergency or standby conductors crossing the S/V ownership line.
- The draft also raises feeding V standby loads from N as an alternative.
  That is an option to evaluate, not an approved change to the target.
- The reported existing S/V arrangement is one generator and one ATS feeding
  both buildings. This has not yet been confirmed against S/V drawings.
- The draft assumes the existing generator and ATS stay with S. Their actual
  locations and deal ownership still need confirmation.
- Load classifications and code applicability remain for the engineer of
  record to verify; the draft's compliance concerns are not confirmed findings.

EXECUTIVE POSITION - PRELIMINARY, NOT NEW DRAWING VERIFICATION
- This is an operational-independence project, not simply a generator purchase:
  the electrical supply and operating arrangements must match the new ownership
  boundaries while maintaining critical-load continuity.
- If the reported shared S/V generator/ATS arrangement is confirmed, separation
  must address ownership, feeder paths, normal-source sensing, and load coverage.
- N must be included in the dependency review. Building-local distribution does
  not establish that its upstream normal and backup sources are independent.
- The drawn normal-power ties between N and S run from N to S, and 3GEN-002N
  appears on both the SWBD #6 (S/V) and DP-5 (N) systems. If confirmed, these
  are boundary crossings the separation must address in addition to the S/V
  normal-source tie at SWBD #6.
- Load classification, measured demand, and starting/transfer requirements must
  drive equipment selection. Neither the 750kW generator label nor switchboard
  and ATS ratings establish spare generator capacity.
- The transition plan must address shared-service duration, equipment access,
  maintenance/fuel responsibilities, outages, and any temporary backup.
- Recommended next milestone: an owner-labeled source-to-load dependency map
  for normal and backup power, supported by field checks and a verified load
  inventory. Use that evidence to compare separation options and plan cutover.
